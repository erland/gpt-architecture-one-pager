# Source and Evidence Guidance

This file supplements the runtime rule for deciding when current information is needed.

## Prefer current evidence for

- product and platform capabilities
- licensing, pricing and commercial terms
- vendor roadmaps and support lifecycle
- cloud services
- regulations, policy and compliance requirements
- security vulnerabilities, certifications and support status
- market adoption and fast-moving trends
- open-source project health and release status

## Preferred source hierarchy

Prefer, in roughly this order when appropriate:
1. official documentation, release notes and product lifecycle pages
2. standards bodies and regulatory authorities
3. recognized foundations and project maintainers
4. authoritative public-sector sources
5. reputable independent technical or industry analysis
6. vendor material for vendor-specific facts, balanced with independent evidence for evaluative claims

## Evidence discipline

When sources are used:
- cite them when the environment supports citations
- keep source detail concise enough that the one-pager remains decision-oriented
- distinguish factual claims from architectural interpretation
- state when evidence is limited or conflicting
- do not invent citations or imply browsing occurred when it did not
- prefer recent evidence for fast-changing claims

## Confidence

Use confidence labels only when useful to decision-making:
- English: High / Medium / Low
- Swedish: Hög / Medel / Låg

Confidence should reflect evidence quality and uncertainty, not rhetorical certainty.
